How it works

Watch the ball. Everything else waits.

Between balls, he tells you his plan. The bowler picks his own length, line and variation based on how new you are, how deep into the innings you've got, what the rate is doing to you, and where you've been scoring — then announces it before he runs in. He still has to land it: accurate bowlers usually do, and everyone drags one down eventually. That slip is where your extras come from, and it's why the announcement is a hint rather than a guarantee.

Your call is the intent — along the ground or over the top — and then the shot itself. If you'd rather set the deliveries yourself, there's an I'll choose link on his plan that hands you the controls.

When it pitches, time slows down. You get about a second of real time to read the length and choose. The ball keeps travelling at its own speed, so the shot still has to be timed — you just aren't rushed into it.

The white ring closes onto the ball. Play when the ring meets the seam; it turns green when you're in the middle of the bat. There's a slim repeat of the same timing along the very top of the screen if you prefer to watch that.

Shots sit around the edge of the field, each where it actually hits — cover on your right, midwicket on your left, straight at the top, behind square down at the corners. They stay on screen the whole time you're batting, greyed until the ball is live, so you can learn the layout and the keys without a ball coming at you. Shots that suit the ball you actually got light up gold once it has pitched. Defend and Leave are on the bar below.

Going big

Keep it off and a middled shot still races along the ground for four, but it will not clear a 68-metre rope. Turn it on and you get the elevation to go over the top — along with a wider margin for error, so a mistimed one goes straight up.

Running

If the ball is fielded out in the deep you get the option of one more. The length of the throw decides whether that was clever.

Why you can't just bully one bowler

Nobody bowls more than four overs, so across twenty you will face all five. Line up the weak links early and you'll be facing the quick with ten to get off the last over.

Reading the field

Every fielder is named on the ground while you're setting up, so you can see where the gaps are before he runs in. The names disappear once you're in your stance — from down there you're watching the ball, not the field.

Placement is real: middle a drive and you'll find the gap and run two, or beat the sweeper for four. Hit the same shot straight to the man and it's a dot however well you timed it. A defensive push is a dot or a single, never more.

Keys

Number keys 0–9 map to the shots around the edge. Space defends, L leaves, B toggles going big, Enter bowls the next one.
